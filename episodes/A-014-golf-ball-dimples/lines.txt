H1|0|Golfers once thought a smooth ball would fly the furthest. They were wrong.
L01|0|In the 1800s, golf balls were made of gutta-percha, a hard tree sap, and new ones came out smooth.
L02|0|But players noticed something odd: old, beaten-up balls flew further than new ones.
L03|0|So makers started hammering dents into brand-new balls.
L04|0|By the early 1900s, those dents had become neat little dimples.
L05|0|Here's the trick: dimples stir up a thin layer of air that clings to the ball for longer.
L06|0|So the wake behind it is smaller, and there's less drag.
L07|0|That's why every golf ball has hundreds of dimples. Smooth would fall short.
L08|0|So... would you still pick the shiny new one?
