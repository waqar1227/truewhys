# A-026 sources (checked 24 Nov 2026)
- Metal pulls heat out of the tongue much faster than the body replaces it (metal is a far better heat conductor than skin), so the
  saliva freezes when the metal is below freezing:
  - Live Science, "Will Your Tongue Really Stick to a Frozen Flagpole?" (Cornell expert quoted) — https://www.livescience.com/32237-will-your-tongue-really-stick-to-a-frozen-flagpole.html
  - How It Works, "Why does a warm tongue stick to a frozen object?" — https://www.howitworksdaily.com/why-does-a-warm-tongue-stick-to-a-frozen-object/
  - Gizmodo, "How your tongue actually can stick to cold metal" — https://gizmodo.com/how-your-tongue-actually-can-stick-to-cold-metal-1677719843
- Wood and plastic are poor heat conductors, so they don't drain heat fast enough to freeze the saliva (same mechanism):
  - Today I Found Out, "Why your tongue sticks to cold metal" — https://www.todayifoundout.com/?p=38228
  - How It Works (above); Live Science (above)
- The frozen saliva locks into tiny pores/irregularities of both the tongue and the metal surface:
  - Gizmodo (above); How It Works (above)
- Pulling can tear off skin from the tongue; the safe fix is pouring warm (not hot) water on the contact point:
  - Live Science (above); Gizmodo (above); Yahoo News, "Can your tongue really get stuck to a pole…?" — https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/tongue-really-stuck-pole-christmas-211039078.html
