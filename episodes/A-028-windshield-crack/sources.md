# A-028 sources (checked 25 Nov 2026)
- Hot water on a frozen windshield can crack it, because the splashed glass heats and expands fast while the rest stays freezing cold
  (worded "can crack", not "always"):
  - USA TODAY fact check (McKenzie Sadeghi, 18 Feb 2021), "Pouring hot water on your windshield could cause cracked or shattered glass":
    "the glass expands at a quick rate only in spots where the hot water was splashed while the rest of the windshield remains freezing cold" — https://www.yahoo.com/news/fact-check-pouring-hot-water-222425664.html
  - Safelite, "Winter Windshield Tips" (2019): "we advise to not use hot water, as this cracks the glass" — https://www.safelite.com/resource-center/auto-experts/winter-windshield-tips
  - Glass Doctor, "Can You Put Hot Water On a Frozen Windshield?": "Rapid temperature changes in glass can cause it to crack" — https://glassdoctor.com/blog/can-you-pour-hot-water-on-a-frozen-windshield
  - YourMechanic (mechanic Q&A): "the sudden heat stress can cause window cracking" — https://www.yourmechanic.com/question/can-cold-weather-crack-your-car-windows-or-windshield
- Glass is slow to move heat (poor conductor), so the surface heats much faster than the inside:
  - Engineering ToolBox, thermal conductivity table: window glass ≈ 0.96–1.05 W/(m·K) vs steel 16–58, aluminium 215 — https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/thermal-conductivity-d_429.html
  - Wikipedia, "Thermal shock": "the exterior surface increases in temperature much faster than the interior"; parts "expanding differently at their respective temperatures" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_shock
- The name is thermal shock (uneven expansion from a sudden temperature change):
  - Wikipedia "Thermal shock" (above: "a common mode of failure when hot meets cold, such as pouring boiling water in a cold glass")
  - SlashGear, "3 Defrosting Techniques You Should Never Use On Your Car's Windshield" (2023) — https://www.slashgear.com/1369802/deice-windshield-lifehacks-dont-work/
- A chip makes it worse (a crack can grow from it):
  - Safelite (above): the drastic temperature change "can cause small chips to crack even further"
  - Capital One Auto Navigator, "What Happens to Your Windshield When the Temperature Changes?" (2022): "Small pits and chips in the glass only exacerbate the problem … turning that little nick into a lengthy crack" — https://www.capitalone.com/cars/learn/managing-your-money-wisely/what-happens-to-your-windshield-when-the-temperature-changes/1853
  - Wikipedia "Thermal shock" (surface flaws as the weak point; general)
- Safe way: plastic scraper, de-icer spray (e.g. 2/3 rubbing alcohol + 1/3 water), defroster warmed gradually (not full blast):
  - Safelite (above); USA TODAY fact check citing AAA (defrost "should not be turned on full blast right away") and Safelite (alcohol mix);
    Glass Doctor (plastic scraper, de-icing solution, "Gradually increase the temperature setting"); Capital One (plastic scraper, gentle defroster).
- Note on lukewarm water (owner's idea): sources disagree (Glass Doctor suggests warm water to soften ice; Safelite/AAA don't recommend water),
  and water can refreeze, so the video recommends scraper / de-icer / defroster only; the description says "never hot water".
