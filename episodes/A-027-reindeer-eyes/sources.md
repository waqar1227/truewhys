# A-027 sources (checked 24 Nov 2026)
- Reindeer tapetum lucidum (the mirror layer behind the retina) is golden in summer and deep blue in winter:
  - Stokkan K-A. et al. (2013), "Shifting mirrors: adaptive changes in retinal reflections to winter darkness in Arctic reindeer", Proc. R. Soc. B — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24174115/
  - National Geographic (Ed Yong), "Why are reindeer eyes golden in summer but blue in winter?" — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/why-are-reindeer-eyes-golden-in-summer-but-blue-in-winter
  - Royal Society news, "Arctic reindeer have clever adaption to see through dark winters" (2013) — https://royalsociety.org/news/2013/arctic-reindeer-eyesight-adaption
- The tapetum reflects light back through the retina (a second chance for the light to be detected):
  - National Geographic (above); UCL opinion (Glen Jeffery, 2022), "How reindeer eyes transform in winter to give them twilight vision" — https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2022/jul/opinion-how-reindeer-eyes-transform-winter-give-them-twilight-vision
- Arctic winter darkness keeps the pupils dilated for long periods; this likely stops fluid draining, raising eye pressure, which squeezes the
  collagen fibres of the tapetum closer together so it reflects blue:
  - Stokkan et al. 2013 (above); National Geographic (above); Science News, "Reindeer eyes change color in winter darkness" — https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/wild-things/reindeer-eyes-change-color-winter-darkness
- Blue reflects ~50 % less light out of the eye; light is scattered inside instead, making the eye more sensitive in the blue Arctic twilight:
  - Royal Society news (above); Christian Science Monitor, "Reindeer eyes turn blue in winter, study finds" — https://www.csmonitor.com/Science/2013/1030/Reindeer-eyes-turn-blue-in-winter-study-finds
  - UCL opinion 2022 (above): winter light in the Arctic is dominated by blue twilight.
- The sun stays below the horizon for weeks in Arctic winter (polar night): National Geographic (above); UCL opinion (above).
