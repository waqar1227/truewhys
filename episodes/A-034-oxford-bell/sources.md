# A-034 sources (checked 25 Nov 2026)
- The Oxford Electric Bell (Clarendon Dry Pile), Clarendon Laboratory, University of Oxford; set up in 1840 (possibly made c. 1825) and has rung
  almost continuously since (short pauses in high humidity); about 10 billion rings; Guinness "world's most durable battery":
  - Wikipedia, "Oxford Electric Bell" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Electric_Bell
  - University of Oxford, Department of Physics history, "Exhibit 1 – The Clarendon Dry Pile" — https://archwww.physics.ox.ac.uk/history/Exhibit1.html
  - Smithsonian Magazine, "The mystery of a continuously functioning battery from 1840" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/mystery-continuously-functioning-battery-1840-180954028/
  - Gizmodo, "Oxford has a bell that's been ringing for 173 years" — https://gizmodo.com/oxford-has-a-bell-thats-been-ringing-for-173-years-486147066
- Two brass bells, each under (on top of) a dry pile; a metal sphere about 4 mm across hangs between them; it is charged by one bell, repelled and
  attracted to the other, and back; oscillates at 2 Hz (every half second): Wikipedia (above); Hackaday, "The bells! The bells! One battery since
  1840" (2018) — https://hackaday.com/2018/10/07/the-bells-the-bells-one-battery-since-1840 ; Oxford Physics exhibit page (above).
- Inaudible because it is behind two layers of glass: Wikipedia (above); Smithsonian (above).
- Pile composition unknown; coated with molten sulfur for insulation (possibly Zamboni piles): Wikipedia (above); Smithsonian (above); Hackaday (above).
- It will eventually stop when the piles' charges equalise (if the clapper does not wear out first): Wikipedia (above); Oxford Physics (above).
- Era markers in the time-lapse: first powered flight 1903, World Wars 1914 and 1939, Moon landing 1969 (general knowledge, in any encyclopedia).
