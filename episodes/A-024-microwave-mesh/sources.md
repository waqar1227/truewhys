# A-024 sources (checked 23 Nov 2026)
- Household microwave ovens run at 2450 MHz → wavelength ≈ 12 cm; visible light 390–700 nm:
  - Mental Floss, "What's the purpose of the mesh grate in a microwave?" — https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/physics/mesh-grate-microwave-purpose
  - Sciencing, "What's That Mesh On The Inside Of Your Microwave" — https://www.sciencing.com/1834813/whats-mesh-inside-microwave-door-function-explained
  - Physics check: c / f = 3×10^8 / 2.45×10^9 = 0.122 m.
- The metal mesh reflects microwaves back into the oven; the holes are much smaller than the microwave wavelength (design rule: far below 1/10
  of the wavelength) so the waves can't pass, while light passes:
  - US Patent 3,544,751, "Microwave oven having meshing microwave door seal" — https://patents.google.com/patent/US3544751A/en
  - Mental Floss + Sciencing (above); University of Alaska Fairbanks physics notes on microwave safety — https://ffden-2.phys.uaf.edu/631fall2008_web.dir/ness_S/safety1.html
- Light/microwave size ratio: 0.122 m / 500 nm ≈ 244 000 → "hundreds of thousands of times smaller".
- Glass alone does not block microwaves (that is why the door needs the mesh): Sciencing + UAF notes (above).
- Leakage is regulated (FDA, since 1971; ovens are safe), description only: FDA "Microwave Ovens" — https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens
