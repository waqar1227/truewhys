# A-030 sources (checked 25 Nov 2026)
- Many mini light strings are wired in series: one path, current must pass through every bulb; one burnt-out (open) bulb stops the whole string:
  - U.S. Department of Energy, "How Do Holiday Lights Work?": "Electricity must pass from one light to the next for the entire strand to light up.
    When a filament in one bulb blows out, it creates an open circuit" — https://www.energy.gov/node/997476
    (archived: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20170108153145/https://www.energy.gov/articles/how-do-holiday-lights-work)
  - Michigan State University Extension, "Why did my string of holiday lights go out?" (series wiring, one bad, loose or missing bulb darkens the
    string) — https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/why_did_my_string_of_holiday_lights_go_out
  - HowStuffWorks, "How Christmas Lights Work" (series strings, shunts) — https://people.howstuffworks.com/culture-traditions/holidays-christmas/christmas-lights.htm/printable
- Inside each incandescent mini bulb a thin wire (filament) glows; when it breaks the loop opens:
  - energy.gov (above); HowStuffWorks (above).
- Newer bulbs have a shunt: a bypass wire wrapped across the lead wires below the filament; when the filament burns out the shunt lets current
  pass so the rest stays lit:
  - energy.gov (above): newer bulbs "contain an internal shunt … to provide a bypass path for current".
  - US patent 4,727,449 "Filament bypass circuit" (shunt wire for series light sets) — https://patents.google.com/patent/US4727449
  - HowStuffWorks (above).
- A loose or missing bulb still breaks the loop (the shunt is inside the bulb, so an empty socket has no bypass):
  - energy.gov (above): popping one out of its socket "still darkens the whole strand".
  - MSU Extension (above): "one bad, loose or missing lightbulb will make the whole string go dark".
