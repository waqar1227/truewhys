H1|0|Why can one bulb turn off the whole string of lights?
L01|0|One tiny bulb fails... and suddenly every light on the string goes dark.
L02|0|Many light strings are wired in series. That's one single loop, and the power has to flow through every bulb in a row.
L03|0|Inside each bulb, a thin wire glows. If it snaps, the loop is broken, and the power stops for all of them.
L04|0|That's why newer bulbs hide a tiny bypass called a shunt. When the wire burns out, the power jumps across it and keeps going.
L05|0|But a loose or missing bulb still breaks the loop... so push that one back in, and the whole string lights up.
