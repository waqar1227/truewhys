# A-033 sources (checked 25 Nov 2026)
- The rule: "Regardless of whether smoking is allowed in any other part of the airplane, lavatories must have self-contained, removable ashtrays
  located conspicuously on or near the entry side of each lavatory door" (also: "Smoking is not allowed in lavatories"):
  - 14 CFR § 25.853(f)–(g), Cornell LII — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.853
  - eCFR, 14 CFR 25.853 — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-25/subpart-D/subject-group-ECFR1e1f52030ba4797/section-25.853
- Why: to give a person who still has lit smoking material an obvious place to put it out before entering, instead of a waste bin with paper:
  - Marketplace (23 Feb 2024), "Why do airplanes have ashtrays on board if smoking is banned?" (quotes the FAA's "obvious location to dispose of
    smoking materials" and a former cabin crew/firefighter on bins with "other combustibles") — https://www.marketplace.org/story/2024/02/23/why-do-airplanes-have-ashtrays-on-board-if-smoking-is-banned
  - CNN Money (9 Mar 2017), "Why airplanes still have ashtrays" — https://money.cnn.com/2017/03/09/news/airplane-ashtrays/
  - FAA Amendment 25-74 (1991), lavatory fire protection after lavatory fires (waste bins' fire containment worn in service) — https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/FRDocket25774amend25-74.pdf
- Smoking banned on all US domestic and international flights to/from the US from 2000 (1988: ≤2 h flights; 1990: ≤6 h): Marketplace (above);
  Wikipedia, "Smoking on aircraft" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_on_aircraft
- Each lavatory has a smoke detector system, and a built-in fire extinguisher for each towel/paper/waste receptacle that discharges automatically
  into it on a fire: 14 CFR § 25.854(a)–(b), Cornell LII — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.854 ; FAA Amendment 25-74 (above).
- Not claimed: specific accidents or casualty numbers; "every US flight" refers to the 2000 US law (flights to/from the US).
