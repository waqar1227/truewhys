# B-008 sources (checked 26 Nov 2026; every claim 2+ sources; disputed details hedged or left out)

## Cold open + #2 Microwave oven (Percy Spencer, Raytheon, 1945)
- Spencer at Raytheon building magnetrons for radar; candy bar melted in his pocket (1945); popcorn and an egg tests; patent filed 8 Oct 1945 (US 2,495,429, granted 1950);
  1947 Radarange ~6 ft tall, ~750 lb; countertop home model 1967 (Amana RR-4) = "twenty more years":
  APS News, "This Month in Physics History: October 8, 1945: First patent for microwave cooking" (2015) · IEEE Spectrum, "A Brief History of the Microwave Oven" ·
  History.com, "The Microwave Oven Was Invented by Accident" (Crystal Ponti) · Lemelson-MIT, "Percy Spencer" · Library of Congress Inside Adams blog, "Microwaves" (2021).
- Spencer never finished grammar school, self-taught; leading expert on radar tube design: Lemelson-MIT · Wikipedia "Percy Spencer" (cites Raytheon/Osepchuk).
- Hedge: colleagues remembered a gradual discovery by several people (John Osepchuk, 1984) and the candy bar is called a legend/anecdote → script says
  "So the story goes" and "his colleagues remembered lots of small tests, not one magic moment": Wikipedia "Percy Spencer" · The Takeout, "The original microwave ovens…" · IEEE Spectrum.
- Microwaves heat food by making water molecules oscillate: IEEE Spectrum · APS News.

## #7 Slinky (Richard James, 1943–1945)
- Naval engineer at a Philadelphia shipyard (Cramp), springs to steady ship instruments, a spring fell from a shelf and "walked" down; Betty James found the name in a dictionary;
  Gimbels Philadelphia, Nov 1945, 400 at $1 sold out in ~90 minutes; hundreds of millions sold since:
  Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia, "Slinky" · Smithsonian Magazine, "The Accidental Invention of the Slinky" · PBS American Experience, "The Slinky" · Time, "The Slinky at 70" (2015) · Lemelson-MIT, "Richard James" · Wikipedia "Slinky".
- Left out: exact sales totals (20,000 vs 22,000 in 1945 differ); anything about his later life.

## #6 Hook-and-loop (George de Mestral, 1941)
- 1941 walk with his dog, burrs, microscope showed tiny hooks catching loops; years of work, cotton failed, nylon worked; name from velours + crochet:
  WIPO, "From a Mountain Walk to 2,100 Patents: The Velcro IP Story" · Lemelson-MIT, "George de Mestral" · History.com, "Velcro invention" · McGill Office for Science and Society, "Under the microscope: Velcro".
- Hedge: one source (APS) dates the walk to 1948; most (WIPO, Lemelson, History.com) give 1941 → we use 1941, no patent dates spoken (they differ 1951/1954/1955).

## #5 Super glue (Harry Coover, Eastman Kodak, 1942 / 1951 / 1958)
- 1942 searching for clear plastic gun sights, cyanoacrylates too sticky, rejected; 1951 Joyner and Shearer measuring refractive index, the refractometer prisms bonded;
  no heat or pressure needed; moisture on surfaces triggers polymerisation; sold as Eastman 910 in 1958; bonds skin:
  Lemelson-MIT, "Harry Coover" · Wikipedia "Harry Coover" · Popular Science, "How a shot at making better gunsights became Super Glue instead" · CBS News Almanac, "Super glue" ·
  Science Focus, "How does super glue work?" (water/hydroxide ions) · Hobart and William Smith Colleges, "Harry W. Coover Jr."

## #4 Non-stick PTFE (Roy Plunkett, DuPont, 6 April 1938)
- Tetrafluoroethylene refrigerant gas in cylinders; nothing came out but weight said full; cut open, white waxy solid; polymerised to PTFE; patent 1941; trademark 1945;
  fluorine-wrapped chains, very slippery, almost nothing sticks; non-stick cookware coating:
  Science History Institute, "Roy J. Plunkett" · APS News, "April 1938: Discovery of Teflon" (2021) · Lemelson-MIT, "Roy Plunkett" · Britannica, "Roy Plunkett" · EBSCO Research Starters.
- We say "non-stick coating", never a brand on screen.

## #3 Laminated safety glass (Edouard Benedictus, 1903 / 1909)
- 1903 dropped flask that had held cellulose nitrate; film held the shards in the flask's shape; film between two glass sheets, patent 1909; first wide use in WWI gas-mask eyepieces,
  then cars; modern windscreens are laminated and crack in a web but stay in one piece:
  Wikipedia "Laminated glass" · McGill Office for Science and Society (node 8774) · Britannica video "Invention of safety glass by Edouard Benedictus" · Today I Found Out (2011) ·
  American Ceramic Society Bulletin, "The evolution and importance of car windshields" · Car Design News, "Flashback: designing the car windscreen".
- Left out: the story that he read about a specific car crash (only blogs); any law dates (one source only).

## #1 Penicillin (Alexander Fleming, Sept 1928; Florey and Chain, Oxford, 1940; Nobel 1945)
- Returned from holiday Sept 1928 to staphylococcus dishes left on the bench; mould colony with a clear zone; named it penicillin; hard to purify, little used for a decade:
  Science History Institute, "Alexander Fleming" · PBS NewsHour, "The real story behind the world's first antibiotic" · Live Science, "Science history: Alexander Fleming…" (2023) · PubMed 15175995 "Penicillin: its discovery and early development".
- 1940 Oxford team (Florey, Chain) showed it cures infections; mass production by the end of the war; Nobel Prize 1945 shared by Fleming, Florey and Chain; antibiotics have saved many millions of lives:
  University of Oxford, "Penicillin: the Oxford story" · Sir William Dunn School of Pathology, "The Discovery of Penicillin" · Science History Institute, "Howard Walter Florey and Ernst Boris Chain" ·
  University of Cambridge Biochemistry, "Ernst Boris Chain" · American Chemical Society, International Historic Chemical Landmark "Discovery and Development of Penicillin".
- Left out: the famous Fleming quote (no primary source found); mouse-test details (no animal harm shown); exact lives-saved figures (estimates vary).
