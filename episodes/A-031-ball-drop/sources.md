# A-031 sources (checked 25 Nov 2026)
- Sailors found longitude by comparing local (sun) time with a chronometer kept on home-port (Greenwich) time; clock errors = position errors:
  - Royal Museums Greenwich, "Using time to solve longitude: the timekeeper method" (2014) — https://rmg.co.uk/stories/blog/time-solve-longitude-timekeeper-method
  - Wikipedia, "Marine chronometer": "The time difference between the chronometer and the ship's local time can be used to calculate the longitude"
    — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_chronometer
  - Smithsonian "Time and Navigation" — https://timeandnavigation.si.edu/node/176
- "One minute wrong could mean fifteen miles off": the Earth turns 15° per hour, so 1 minute = 0.25° of longitude = 15 nautical miles at the
  equator (1° = 60 nm); less at higher latitudes, hence "could" (arithmetic from the two sources above; RMG longitude pages).
- 1833: the Royal Observatory, Greenwich put up a time ball; it drops at exactly 1 pm every day so ships on the Thames could set their chronometers:
  - Royal Museums Greenwich, "Greenwich Time Ball: one time for all" — https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/topics/greenwich-time-ball-one-time-all
    (the ball has dropped at 1 pm daily since 1833 and still operates)
  - Wikipedia, "Time ball" (Greenwich 1833, Capt. Robert Wauchope's idea; ships waited for the 1 pm drop) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_ball
  - Kinns, "The early history of the time ball" (J. Astron. Hist. Heritage 2010) — https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/2010JAHH...13..194K
- 1907: New York's first New Year's Eve ball drop (31 Dec 1907, into 1908), borrowed from the maritime time-ball idea; an illuminated ball of iron
  and wood with 100 light bulbs, dropped at midnight:
  - Smithsonian Magazine, "The first ever Times Square ball drop was held … on this day in 1907" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/the-first-ever-times-square-ball-drop-was-held-atop-the-new-york-times-headquarters-on-this-day-in-1907-starting-a-cherished-tradition-180985753/
  - NPR, "Why do we drop a ball on New Year's Eve? The Times Square tradition, explained" (31 Dec 2024) — https://www.npr.org/2024/12/31/nx-s1-5235245/new-years-eve-ball-drop-times-square
  - Times Square Alliance, "History of New Year's Eve & the Times Square Ball" — https://www.timessquarenyc.org/nye/nye-history-times-square-ball
- Not claimed (unverified): a "fireworks ban" as the reason; the video only says New York borrowed the idea.
