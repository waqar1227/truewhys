# B-004 C2 (cut Short, rewritten). Frame 1: a big YELLOW stop sign, 1920s driver squinting at it. Answer (fading red) in the last 5 s; loops to the yellow sign.
H1|0|Why were stop signs once yellow?
L01|0|In 1923, American road engineers made a rule: the more sides, the more danger.
L02|0|A circle for railway crossings. Eight sides for stop.
L03|0|And for about thirty years, most stop signs were yellow, with black letters.
L04|0|Red would be better. So why wait until 1954?
L05|0|Because red paint faded in the sun. And a pale pink stop sign is easy to miss.
