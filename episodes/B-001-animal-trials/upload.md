In 1386, a pig was dressed in a man's clothes and executed after a real court trial. It wasn't a joke — for centuries, European courts put animals on trial.

Pigs, bulls, rats, a rooster and even weevils got summonses, lawyers and verdicts. We follow the Pig of Falaise, the lawyer who defended rats (and blamed the cats), the rooster burned for laying an egg, and the weevils who were offered their own land — then reveal the surprising reason church courts kept holding these trials.
