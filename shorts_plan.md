## Beat scripts (v1, ~95–110 words each; sources checked at script stage, 2+ per claim, in each `sources.md`)

**A-001 Quicksand.** Hook: "If you step into quicksand, you won't vanish like in the movies." Setup: quicksand is sand so soaked
with water the grains stop gripping each other. Zoom-in: grains + water cutaway; it's about twice as dense as your body, so you
float and sink only to about your waist. Twist: it grips; pulling a foot out fast takes about the force needed to lift a car
(Bonn et al., Nature 2005). Payoff: don't yank: wiggle slowly to let water in, lean back, spread out, float to the edge.
Close: "The real danger isn't the sand. It's the tide. Would you stay calm?"

**A-002 Polar bear liver.** Hook: 1597, Dutch sailors stuck in the Arctic cook a polar bear; days later their skin starts to
peel (Gerrit de Veer's journal). Setup: the culprit was the liver. Zoom-in: food chain: fish store vitamin A, seals eat fish,
bears eat seals, it piles up in the bear's liver. Twist: your body can't flush extra vitamin A fast, so a burger-sized portion
can seriously poison an adult (hypervitaminosis A). Payoff: Arctic peoples knew it for centuries and never ate it.
Close: "Would you have known?" (No gore: peeling shown as a flaky cartoon hand, not wounds.)

**A-003 Plane door (Style R).** Hook: "What happens if someone pulls this lever at 35,000 feet?" Setup: the cabin is pumped up to
air like a mountain town, far thicker than the air outside. Zoom-in: X-ray of the door: a plug door, wider than its frame, must
move inward before it can open out. Twist: the pressure presses it into the frame with more than 10 tonnes, about two
elephants. Payoff: nobody's arms can win; many doors also lock at speed. Close: "Still nervous sitting next to it?"
(Safety-positive: shows why it cannot happen; no instructions.)

**A-004 Tickling.** Hook: "Try to tickle yourself. Nothing happens. Why?" Setup: tickling needs surprise. Zoom-in: inside the
brain, the cerebellum predicts every move you make and turns the feeling down. Twist: scientists built a robot that copied
people's own hand moves; add a delay of a fifth of a second and they started to feel tickled again
(Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith). Payoff: your brain cancels what it expects so it notices the world. Close: "So who's ticklish here?"

**A-005 Brain freeze.** Hook: Wy's head freezes. Setup: ice cream hits the roof of your mouth. Zoom-in (inside the head):
cold palate → blood vessels nearby squeeze, then suddenly widen; a big artery behind the eyes swells with blood. Twist: the
pain nerve there also serves your forehead, so your brain thinks the pain is in your forehead. Payoff: press your tongue to
the roof of your mouth, or sip something warm, and it fades in seconds. Close: "Your brain isn't frozen. So what is it feeling?"

**A-006 Castle toilets.** Hook: soldiers climbing up a toilet chute. Setup: castle toilets (garderobes) were small rooms on the
outer wall with a stone seat over a chute that dropped outside, often into the moat. Zoom-in: cutaway of the wall and chute.
Twist: in 1204 at Château Gaillard, accounts say attackers got in through a latrine chute (told as "accounts say", a disputed
detail). Payoff: castles later added bars and bends to chutes; some say clothes were hung in garderobes to keep moths away.
Close: "Would you have climbed it?" (Kept clean: no filth close-ups.)

**A-007 Square windows.** Hook: crack spreading from a square corner. Setup: in 1954 two Comet jets, the first jet airliners,
broke apart in the air. Zoom-in: stress lines flowing around a sharp corner pile up there. Twist: investigators pressurised a
whole Comet in a water tank again and again until its skin cracked at the corner of a square-cornered cut-out.
Payoff: round corners spread the stress, so every window since has been rounded. Close: "So what other shapes are hiding
for a reason?" (Respectful: no victims shown, no crash footage, wording factual.)
