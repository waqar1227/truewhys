### 1c. What the hits have in common (our reading of the snapshot + sources)
1. **One clear situation in the first second** (a title like "That Was NOT Chocolate…" + a picture that already shows the problem).
2. **Recurring, instantly readable characters** (Boggs, Goodland's food, Pencilmate, Oddbods): viewers know the "rules" of the world, so the twist lands faster.
3. **Wordless or nearly wordless** for the big global channels (Frame Order, Oddbods, CRACKÉ, Rollin' Wild): body language + sound effects.
4. **A twist ending + a final "button"** (an extra small joke after the twist) that makes people rewatch or comment.
5. **Steady volume** (2 – 7 per week) from studios with teams. A solo/automated channel cannot match volume, so it must win on quality.
6. Two of the biggest (Cas, part of Pencilmation) use **other people's famous characters**. We will never do that (copyright + owner rules).

## 3. Risks and policies

### 3a. "Inauthentic" (old name "repetitious") content — the biggest risk for a cartoon series
- YouTube's monetisation policy page lists as not monetisable: mass-produced or repetitive content, **"templated storylines"**,
  slideshows with little narrative; the rule applies to the **whole channel** ("monetization may be removed from your entire channel")
  ([YouTube Help: Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en), read 13 Nov 2026).
- Renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025; YouTube called it a minor update to an existing rule
  ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/),
  [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660)).
  A further clarification was reported in July 2026 ([Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)).
- Secondary summaries say the examples include **characters repeating the same situation with the same outcome**
  ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/), [vidIQ reused-content guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/)).
  → A recurring cast is fine (every big channel above has one), but **every Short needs a new situation and a new twist**. Never a fill-in-the-blank template.

### 3b. Monetisation (YouTube Partner Program)
- Full ad revenue: **1,000 subs + 10 M valid public Shorts views in 90 days** (or 4,000 watch hours in 12 months).
  Lower tier (fan funding/Shopping only, some countries): 500 subs + 3 public uploads + 3 M Shorts views in 90 days
  ([vidIQ YPP guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-guide/), [AIR](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)).
- Reported but **not confirmed on YouTube's own pages**: from 1 Feb 2027 the bar may rise to 8,000 hours or 20 M Shorts views
  ([NewscastStudio, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/)). Check before relying on it.
- Money is small: entertainment/comedy Shorts usually earn **about $0.01 – $0.05 per 1,000 views** (vendor estimates; no official figure)
  ([AIR 274-channel data](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026),
  [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-rpm-2026)). 10 M views ≈ $100 – $500. Growth and a long-form path matter more than Shorts ads.

### 3c. Made for Kids (COPPA)
- A cartoon is **not automatically "for kids"**; the FTC looks at subject, visuals, characters, music, language, ads and real audience age.
  If children are one intended audience and the factors point to kids, it is "made for kids" (mixed audience counts)
  ([YouTube Help: Determining if your content is Made for Kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en-GB),
  [vidIQ COPPA summary](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/is-your-youtube-content-made-for-children-ftc-coppa),
  [FPF COPPA myths](https://fpf.org/blog/coppa4creators/)).
- Made-for-kids videos lose personalised ads, **comments**, the notification bell, end screens and more; each video can be appealed once.
  Disney reportedly paid $10 M to the FTC over wrong labels (same sources).
- YouTube's systems may set a cartoon as "for kids" by themselves (Cas example, §1b).
- → **Decision needed:** we aim at teens and adults (13+) with grown-up, everyday-life situations (work, phones, diets, queues, wifi),
  not nursery themes or toy-like visuals; we answer the audience question **honestly on every upload**. If a premise is really for small
  children, we either skip it or mark it "made for kids".

### 3d. Copyright and sound
- Only CC0 or self-made SFX and laughs; no clips from TV laugh tracks, no famous cartoon sounds, no parody of other people's characters.
- Mixkit sounds use the Mixkit licence (not CC0); some laugh tracks online are CC-BY (credit needed) or paid (Artlist, Uppbeat, Motion Array)
  ([Mixkit funny SFX](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/funny/), [BudgetPixel](https://budgetpixel.com/sfx/sitcom-audience-laugh-9839be41)).
  Freesound has CC0 laughter ([Freesound forum](https://freesound.org/forum/freesound-project/44349)); search it with the CC0 filter.
- Note: from this research session the Freesound API answered **403** (13 Nov 2026); the production session should run `python lib/sfx_fetch.py`
  as usual and tell the owner if the key needs renewing.

### 3e. Ethics (CLAUDE.md, unchanged)
No hate or mockery of groups, nothing about any religion (no religious places, clothing or figures as jokes), no gore or death for shock
(a chase ends in a splash, a bonk, a ruined hat — never blood), no harmful "how-to" (no copyable dangerous stunts), no AI labels or AI mentions in public text.

## 4. Production fit (what we already have)

| Need | We have | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| 2D characters with expressions | `lib/cast.py`: vector cut-out puppets, head/body/arm layers, **8 expressions, 4 mouth shapes, blinks**, many animals (pig, rat, cat, rooster, dog, bull, horse…) | New comedy cast drawn to a rounder, "squash and stretch" design; extra comedy expressions (shock "take", smug, panic, dizzy, sheepish); walk/run cycles |
| Flat 2D on CPU | A-008 (owner-made flat 2D Short, 1080×1920) proves CPU 2D is publishable; compositing in `lib/compose.py`, camera moves in `lib/camera.py` | A "Style F" comedy timing toolkit: holds, anticipation, overshoot, smear frames, camera shake, speed lines, dust puff, impact stars, sweat drops |
| Sound | `library/sfx` (CC0, mapped in `lib/audio.py`), procedural music (`lib/music.py`, has a "playful" mood) | Cartoon SFX pack (boing, slide whistle up/down, zip, splat, bonk, honk, record scratch, cymbal "ba-dum-tss", tiny giggles, crowd laugh) — CC0 or made in code with numpy |
| Voice | Gemini TTS, Kokoro, espeak-ng | Wordless "gibberish" voices (pitched grunts, gasps) made from Kokoro/espeak or self-recorded; no narrator |
| 3D | Style T on Kaggle GPU | Not needed for comedy; keep GPU hours for TrueWhys |

**Realistic output at full quality:** start with **3 Shorts per week** (weeks 1 – 4), rise to 4 – 5 per week once the cast rigs,
timing toolkit and SFX pack exist. Quality first, as for TrueWhys.

**Keeping usage low:** one batch session per week (write 3 – 5 premises → animatic → render all on CPU → one contact-sheet QA);
reuse sets and character rigs; scripts for timing and sound; AI only for jokes, storyboards and QA.
