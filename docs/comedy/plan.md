# Comedy cartoon Shorts — plan and start guide (13 Nov 2026)

Plan only. Nothing is produced until the owner says "start". Sources and numbers: `docs/comedy/research.md`.

## 1. Recommendation in one paragraph
Make it a **new, separate YouTube channel**. TrueWhys promises real facts; made-up comedy on the same channel would confuse viewers and
YouTube's recommendations. The format is proven: wordless or nearly wordless 2D cartoon gag channels with a recurring cast (Frame Order,
GOODLAND, Pencilmation, Land of Boggs, Oddbods) get hundreds of thousands to millions of views per Short. But two risks are real and
must be designed in from day one: (1) **"inauthentic / templated" content** — every Short needs a new situation and a new twist,
never a fill-in template; (2) **"made for kids"** — cartoon animals drift to kids, which turns off comments and most ads, so we aim
at teens and adults with everyday grown-up situations and answer the audience question honestly. Money from Shorts alone is small
(about $0.01 – $0.05 per 1,000 views), so measure success by views, swipe rate and subscribers first.

## 2. Positioning
- **Line:** "Tiny cartoons. Big plans. Bigger twists."
- **Promise:** 15 – 35 second, wordless cartoon stories where the plan backfires, the tables turn, and the last second makes you replay it.
- **Audience:** teens and adults worldwide (no language needed). Everyday adult life: jobs, queues, diets, phones, neighbours, gym, holidays.
- **Not:** facts (that is TrueWhys), parody of famous characters, nursery themes, mean humour about any group, anything religious.

## 4. Recurring cast (original; final designs made by the production session)
Rule: **anyone can win.** The winner changes from Short to Short, so stories never become "same situation, same outcome".
1. **Pip** — a tiny, smug sparrow with a clipboard. Always has a "perfect plan". Colour: lemon yellow.
2. **Snap** — a lanky crocodile who believes he is a master of disguise (he is not). Wants Pip's snacks, never Pip. Colour: lime green, purple hat.
3. **Moe** — a huge, gentle hippo who just wants a quiet day and wins by accident. Colour: lilac.
4. **Nana Shell** — an old tortoise who is slow, sweet and secretly always three steps ahead. Often the final "button". Colour: teal shell, pink cardigan.
World: a sunny animal town (market, park, beach, gym, café, bus stop) — no religious buildings or symbols.

## 5. The house format (every Short)
1. **0 – 1 s HOOK:** the problem is visible in the first frame (no title cards, no logo intro).
2. **SETUP → ESCALATE (rule of three):** try 1, try 2 bigger, try 3 biggest — each step further from normal.
3. **TWIST:** the tables turn in a way that was fair (planted earlier) but unexpected.
4. **BUTTON:** one more tiny joke in the last 1 – 2 s; the last frame cuts cleanly back to the first frame (loop).
- Length 15 – 35 s (the data favours 11 – 30 s). Wordless: grunts, gasps, gibberish, at most 3 on-screen words.
- Cartoon SFX on every action (CC0 or made in code), a short giggle/laugh sting at the button only, light procedural music.
- Expressions do the talking: big anticipation, a "take" (shock face) before every twist, holds of 6 – 12 frames before punches.
- Title: 3 – 7 words that tease the twist + 1 emoji (e.g. pattern "He Thought It Was Safe… 😳"), 3 – 5 hashtags. Thumbnail = hook frame.
- End: the channel's own 1.5 s end card ("Follow for more twists") — not Wy (Wy belongs to TrueWhys).
- Comment bait that is honest: a question in the description or pinned comment ("Who really won? 🐢 or 🐦"). Never fake engagement.

## 6. Thirty original premises (one line each: setup → twist)
1. Snap hides as a bush to grab Pip's sandwich → a goat gardener trims him into a poodle shape; Pip booked the gardener.
2. Pip builds a perfect cake trap for Snap → Nana eats the cake so slowly the trap rusts, then it springs on Pip.
3. Moe sits on a "WET PAINT" bench and won't move all day → the paint dries, he's declared a sculpture and wins first prize.
4. Snap chases Pip into a lift → the lift's weight alarm goes off; Moe steps in and the two must share a very tight ride down.
5. Pip "reserves" the best beach spot with one tiny towel → Moe lies down on it without seeing him; Pip finally gets the shade he wanted.
6. Snap disguises himself as a delivery driver to steal Pip's pizza → Pip tips him so well that Snap feels too guilty and delivers it.
7. Nana is last in a long queue → she opens a second till herself; she owns the shop.
8. Pip sets 10 alarms to beat Snap to the free-doughnut sale → the alarms wake Moe, who gets there first and shares one crumb with Pip.
9. Snap pretends to be a statue in the park → pigeons treat him like a statue too; Pip hands him an umbrella.
10. Moe tries to sneak one cookie at night with spy moves → the fridge light reveals everyone else already inside the fridge.
11. Pip's paper plane message to Snap says "TRUCE" → it lands on Nana, who folds it into a better plane and gets the snacks.
12. Snap enters a "best disguise" contest as Pip → the judges can't find him; he wins, but nobody believes it was him.
13. Moe on a tiny bicycle can't go uphill → he finally gets up; the hill was the queue for an ice-cream van that just drove away.
14. Pip makes a "no crocodiles" sign for his picnic → Snap arrives as "definitely a log"; Nana sits on the log; the log sighs.
15. Snap orders a tiny diet salad → it arrives on a plate made of cake.
16. Pip races Nana and naps halfway (he knows how it ends) → he wakes up and wins, but Nana was never racing; she was walking to the finish-line café.
17. Moe's selfie keeps getting photobombed by Snap → in the final photo, Snap is the one photobombed by a giant Moe yawn.
18. Pip trains a guard puppy to keep Snap away → the puppy loves Snap; now Snap guards Pip's house too.
19. Snap tries the "fake injury" trick to get a seat on the bus → Nana gives him her seat, then her walking stick, then her knitting; he's stuck knitting.
20. Pip hides his snack under a cup and shuffles three cups → Snap picks right every time; Pip didn't notice the cups are glass.
21. Moe tries to be quiet in the library → his stomach rumble knocks a shelf over; the books land in perfect alphabetical order.
22. Snap installs a fake "Free Wi-Fi" sign to lure Pip → everyone in town arrives; Snap ends up working the help desk.
23. Pip builds a sandcastle fortress against the tide → the tide stops just short; Moe cannonballs into the sea.
24. Nana's slow parallel parking holds up traffic for a minute → the car behind honks, then sees it was a 20-car tow chain she's pulling.
25. Snap disguises himself as a mailbox → Pip posts a letter into his mouth: a party invitation for Snap.
26. Moe tries the gym's tiny weights to look strong → he lifts the whole weights rack by accident while looking for the water fountain.
27. Pip invents a robot vacuum to clean his nest → it hoovers up Snap's disguise kit, then Snap's hat, then chases Snap out of town.
28. Snap bets he can stay hidden in a game of hide-and-seek → nobody looks for him; he finds them having cake without him, but they saved him a slice.
29. Pip tries to get Moe off his favourite swing with three clever tricks → the swing breaks; Moe is happily sitting on the grass where the swing was, and Pip has nowhere.
30. Nana wins every board game → the camera pulls back: she's playing against three mirrors, and still cheating.
(Before production, each premise gets a 6-panel storyboard and an ethics check; weak ones are cut, never padded.)

## 7. First month (after "start"; 3 Shorts a week at full quality)
- **Week 0 (setup, no uploads):** cast designs and rigs, comedy timing toolkit, SFX pack, end card, logo/banner, 3 pilot Shorts.
  Owner watches the 3 pilots and says yes / change. Nothing goes up before that.
- **Weeks 1 – 4:** 3 Shorts per week, **Wednesday, Friday, Sunday at 21:30 UTC** (= 4:30 pm New York, 9:30 pm London; research favours
  late afternoon US, Friday strongest). Scheduled as private + publish time, like TrueWhys.
  - Week 1: premises 1, 3, 6 (one per main character type: disguise, accident-win, kindness twist)
  - Week 2: 8, 12, 15
  - Week 3: the best type from weeks 1 – 2 again (new premise), plus 2 others
  - Week 4: 2 of the best type, 1 new test
- TikTok: optional, later (needs a second Metricool brand; check if the free plan allows it).

## 8. How we measure (our own starting thresholds, not industry rules)
Numbers from YouTube Studio → Content → each Short (owner pastes them, or the production session reads the API where possible).
| Check | GROW (do more of it) | CHANGE (fix hook/look) | STOP or rethink |
|---|---|---|---|
| After 2 weeks (6 Shorts) | ≥ 2 Shorts over 10 k views and "Viewed" ≥ 65 % | "Viewed" under 55 % on most Shorts → change first-second hook and thumbnails | — (too early) |
| After 4 weeks (12 Shorts) | Median ≥ 5 k views, one Short ≥ 100 k, subscribers growing every week → go to 4 – 5 a week | Median 1 – 5 k → keep the best character/premise type, drop the rest | Median under 1 k and no Short over 10 k after the changes → pause and discuss with the owner |
Also watch: average % viewed (aim 80 %+, loops can pass 100 %), subscribers per 1,000 views, and any video auto-set to "made for kids".

## 9. START GUIDE for the owner (simple steps)
**Step 1 — Pick the name.** Choose one of: Turnabout Toons, Topsy Tails, Boingtown (or another from §3).

**Step 2 — Make the channel (10 minutes, on your phone or laptop).**
1. Open YouTube, signed in with the same Google account you use for TrueWhys.
2. Tap your picture → Settings → "Add or manage your channel(s)" → "Create a channel".
3. Type the name. This makes a new channel next to TrueWhys (you switch between them from your picture).
4. In YouTube Studio → Customisation → Basic info → Handle: type the handle (e.g. @turnabouttoons). Publish.
5. YouTube Studio → Settings → Channel → Advanced settings → Audience: "No, set this channel as not made for kids"
   (our videos are for teens and adults). Save.

**Step 3 — Branding.** The production session makes the logo, banner and channel description. You upload logo and banner in
YouTube Studio → Customisation → Branding (2 minutes).

**Step 4 — The upload token for the NEW channel.** Do the same steps as **guide Part 10.4** (the YouTube token steps you used for
TrueWhys), with two differences:
1. When Google asks you to choose an account or channel, choose the **new comedy channel**, not TrueWhys.
2. Save the new token as a **new** key named `COMEDY_YT_REFRESH_TOKEN`. Do **not** replace `YT_REFRESH_TOKEN` (that one is TrueWhys).
The same `YT_CLIENT_ID` and `YT_CLIENT_SECRET` can be used. Never paste the token into a chat; put it only in the environment
settings, like the other keys. If you cannot find guide Part 10, tell the production session and it will write the steps for you.

**Step 5 — A home for the files.** On GitHub, make a new **private** empty repository named after the channel
(e.g. `turnabout-toons`) and add it to your Claude environment. (Simpler but less tidy: say "use a folder in TrueWhys".)

**Step 6 — Send this to the production session:**
> "Start the comedy channel. Name: ____ . Handle: @____ . Repo: ____ (or: use a folder). Token saved as COMEDY_YT_REFRESH_TOKEN.
> Follow docs/comedy/plan.md. Make Week 0 first and send me the 3 pilot Shorts before anything is uploaded."

## 10. What the production session will set up (after the owner says start)
- **New repo** (or `comedy/` folder) with its own `CLAUDE.md`: same ethics, quality-first, keys, upload and "no AI fields" rules as
  TrueWhys; its own audience rule (teens/adults, honest made-for-kids answer each upload); its own timing rules; no Wy.
- **Skill file** `.claude/skills/comedy-shorts/SKILL.md`: the house format (§5), premise → 6-panel storyboard → animatic → final, QA checklist
  (hook in 1 s, rule of three, fair twist, button, loop, no words needed, ethics, sound licences), anti-template check (new situation + new outcome).
- **Library (reusable, listed in an index):** the 4 cast rigs (built on `lib/cast.py` cut-out puppets, more expressions, walk/run cycles),
  a timing toolkit (holds, anticipation, overshoot, smears, shake, speed lines, dust, stars), 4 – 6 town backgrounds,
  a cartoon SFX pack (CC0 or code-made, credits file), giggle/laugh stings, playful music loops, end card.
- **Pipeline:** CPU-only flat 2D (no Kaggle GPU needed), 1080×1920, reuse of `lib/compose.py`, `lib/audio.py`, `lib/youtube.py`
  (pointed at the new token), `uploaded.json`, `qa.md`, `credits.md` per Short.
- **Usage plan:** one weekly batch session (3 Shorts), one small contact sheet for QA, no polling. Results logged in a
  `results.md` (date, title, premise type, character, length, 48 h / 7 d views, viewed %, avg % viewed, subs).
